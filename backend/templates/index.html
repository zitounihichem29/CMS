ORSC Internal Rulebook

Rules, responsibilities and systems that guide the Operations Research Society Club.

PART I

General Rules

Attendance, club room, communication, conduct and quality standards.

+
01 Attendance & Activity

Every member must complete at least one assigned task per week. Weeks with zero output without a valid excuse count as an absence.

Three consecutive unexplained absences result in a formal warning. A fourth triggers a review meeting with the department leader, which may result in removal.

Members on a break due to exams or personal reasons must notify their department leader in advance. Silence is not an excuse.

Each semester, every member must participate in at least 2 to 3 club-wide initiatives or events, not just department-level work.

Beyond assigned tasks, every member must stay active within their department through training, skill-building, or practice relevant to the department's work at least once every 2 to 3 weeks.

Extended inactivity results in a warning, and continued inactivity may lead to removal from the department's group chat and the department itself.

02 Club Room

The club room is a shared, institutional space. It is a privilege, not a right.

Maximum noise level must be kept reasonable at all times. Between 8:00 AM and 2:30 PM, this is strictly enforced — conversations stay calm, music is either off or through headphones only, and no shouting under any circumstances.

After 2:30 PM, moderate volume is acceptable, but you are still inside a university. Neighboring classrooms, offices, and corridors must be respected at all times.

During exam periods, silence is mandatory for the entire day without exception. The club room during exams is for studying only.

Every member is responsible for their own space and their own garbage. Clean up after yourself before you leave.

Full meals are not permitted inside the club room. Light snacks such as juice, chocolate, biscuits, and similar items are acceptable as long as the space is left clean. Full meals must be eaten outside.

No games are allowed inside the club room between 8:00 AM and 2:30 PM unless they are on a personal device with headphones.

After hours, games are acceptable only as a group activity agreed on by everyone in the room and approved by the president, vice president, or an HR member who is present and supervising.

No outsiders are allowed in the room without explicit permission from the president or a department leader.

Personal belongings left in the room are the owner's responsibility. The club is not liable for any loss.

03 Communication & Tasks

All official club communication currently goes through department Messenger group chats. Once the club platform launches, all official communication will transition there.

You only take tasks assigned through your department leader, the president, or the vice president.

Tasks given informally by other members carry no obligation unless one of the authorized leaders has explicitly granted that member authority to assign work.

Members must respond to messages from their department leader within 24 hours on working days.

Personal conflicts must either remain private without affecting club work, or be brought confidentially to HR. The president or vice president is the last resort when HR cannot resolve the matter.

Taking conflicts to public chats or airing them in the club room is not acceptable.

04 Conduct & Boundaries

There is no hierarchy of respect. Every member, leader, and officer is respected equally as a peer and as a friend. The club is meant to remain a family environment.

Boundaries still exist. Interactions must remain professional enough that nobody feels uncomfortable, targeted, or disrespected. Banter is fine; crossing the line is not.

The president and vice president have the final word on club decisions. Disagreement is allowed through the appropriate channels, not through public pushback or ignoring decisions.

05 Deliverables & Quality

Submitted work must meet the minimum quality standard set by the department leader.

Rushed, incomplete, or careless work will be sent back and counts as a missed deadline until corrected.

Deadlines are set by the department leader. Extensions must be requested before the deadline, not after.

PART VI · HEAD ONLY

Leadership Structure & Rules

Leadership roles, expectations, activity management and accountability.

+
01 Leadership Roles

The club is led by the following core figures: President, Vice President, Secretary, DCM Department Leader, HR Department Leader, DPA Department Leader, and ER Department Leader.

Together, these figures form the club's leadership body. Major decisions, replacements, and structural changes go through them collectively.

02 Elections & Appointments

At the start of each year, all leadership positions are filled through a club-wide vote. Every member has a vote, including the candidates themselves.

The president and vice president together hold confirmed agreement over each department leader elected. If they cannot both agree on a result, the matter is discussed openly within the full leadership body.

Previous holders of a position may express a preference for their successor. This is welcomed and considered, but carries no obligation — it is a recommendation, not an appointment.

If a leader steps down, becomes inactive, or is removed during the semester, any member of the leadership body may nominate a replacement candidate.

The president has final say on a mid-semester replacement, but the decision is discussed and agreed upon between the president, vice president, and remaining department leaders. No replacement is made unilaterally.

Mid-semester replacements do not require a club-wide vote, but the new appointment must be announced transparently to all members.

03 General Leader Expectations

Leaders are members first. All general rules apply to them without exception.

Leaders are held to a noticeably higher standard than regular members. Where a member is expected to do their part, a leader is expected to ensure that everyone around them does theirs as well.

A leader who is inactive, disengaged, or consistently unavailable will be addressed faster and more seriously than a regular member in the same situation.

Department leaders are the first line of response for issues within their department, including workload problems, conflicts, quality issues, and attendance concerns.

These issues should not reach the president unless the leader has genuinely exhausted their options first.

If a problem is escalating and the leader needs support, they bring it to HR first. The president and vice president are the last resort, not the first call.

A leader who consistently escalates small problems upward without attempting to handle them internally is not fulfilling the role.

04 Keeping Members Active

Each department leader is fully responsible for the activity level of their department. If members are idle — no tasks, no projects, no active period — it is the leader's job to fill that gap.

During low-task periods, leaders must still keep members engaged through skill-building, internal learning, or collaborative practice relevant to the department.

DCM: color theory exercises, building palettes, practicing layout, and peer skill-sharing between members.

DPA: researching activity ideas, reviewing past event reports, and preparing briefs for upcoming initiatives.

HR: reviewing member logs, checking in on department morale, and preparing for the next recruitment cycle.

ER: mapping potential partners, drafting outreach templates, and staying informed on what other clubs or institutions are doing.

Leaders must check in with each of their members at least once a week, even when nothing urgent is happening.

05 Secretary — Specific Responsibilities

The secretary is responsible for maintaining the club's official records: meeting notes, decisions made by the leadership body, member logs, and formal documents.

Every leadership meeting must produce a written summary, prepared by the secretary and shared with all leaders within 48 hours.

The secretary does not manage a department but sits within the leadership body with equal voice in collective decisions.

The secretary works closely with HR to ensure that logs, reports, and records are consistent and up to date.

06 HR Leader — Specific Responsibilities

The HR leader must be the most visible example of conduct in the entire club. If the standard is not being modeled at the top of HR, it cannot be enforced below.

HR members are the ones actively maintaining logs and reporting to the HR leader. The HR leader's job is to receive, organize, and act on those reports — not to do all the logging themselves.

Every incident, conflict, warning, or strike issued anywhere in the club must be documented by HR, stored by the HR leader, and reported to the president in the monthly summary.

The HR leader takes full responsibility for the accuracy and completeness of those records. Missing or inconsistent logs reflect directly on them.

07 ER Leader — Specific Responsibilities

The ER leader must keep the president personally informed of every active external relationship, ongoing conversation, or potential partnership — not just at monthly summaries, but as things develop.

No external communication goes out without the ER leader's approval. No agreement is made without the president's sign-off.

The ER leader is the club's external face. Their conduct, professionalism, and reliability in outside interactions directly shape how the club is perceived.

08 Leader Strikes & Demotion

Leaders follow the same 5-strike system as all members, with the same semester reset.

Given their responsibilities, certain failures that might earn a member a warning will earn a leader a strike directly. Consistent departmental inactivity, repeated failure to report, or handling conflicts poorly after multiple instances are clear examples.

At strike 5, a leader is reviewed. The review is conducted by the president, vice president, and at least one other department leader not involved in the situation.

Demotion — the person is removed from the leadership role but continues as a regular member.

Removal — if the offenses are serious enough that demotion is not appropriate, the member is removed under the same terms as any other member.

Reinstatement with conditions — when context clearly warrants it, the leader may remain in their role under defined conditions and a probation period agreed upon by the leadership body.

A demoted leader is not treated with less respect than any other member. The demotion is structural, not personal.

A leader cannot use their position to shield themselves from consequences that would apply to any regular member.

PART VII · EXECUTIVE

President & Vice President

Responsibilities, accessibility and commitment to the club.

+
01 Role & Responsibility

The president holds the highest authority in the club, and with it, the highest level of responsibility and accountability.

The vice president shares that responsibility wherever the president is unavailable, in conflict, or has delegated authority. The vice president is not simply a backup — they are a co-pillar of the club's leadership.

Together, the president and vice president are responsible for the overall health, direction, and culture of the club.

02 What the President & Vice President Are Responsible For

Ensuring every department is functioning and every department leader has the support they need.

Making final decisions on leadership replacements, removals, review-level sanctions, and external agreements.

Being present and reachable — not just visible at events, but available when members or leaders genuinely need them.

Reviewing the monthly HR report and making sure necessary action is taken.

Holding the leadership body accountable to the same standards expected from every member.

Setting the club's semester goals, priorities, and expectations.

Protecting the club's reputation inside and outside the university.

03 How to Reach Us

Every member has the right to reach the president or vice president directly. There is no rule against it.

However, members are asked to respect the club's structure. If an issue can be handled by the department leader, it should go there first. If it requires HR, it should go to HR.

If the department leader is the problem, if HR is handling something unfairly, or if the issue is serious enough that the normal structure cannot resolve it, members may come directly to the president or vice president.

No member will face consequences for raising a genuine concern directly when the situation calls for it.

The president and vice president are meant to remain approachable. The structure exists for efficiency, not distance.

04 Our Commitment to the Club

Lead by example.

Be fair in decisions and consequences.

Remain present and available.

Listen before making decisions.

Protect the culture and values of the club.

Be honest and transparent with members.

Carry the club forward and leave it stronger than it was at the beginning of the term.

PART VIII

Strike System

Formal warnings, the five-strike scale, reviews and semester resets.

+

The strike system is a formal mechanism for genuine, intentional, or repeated misconduct. It is not designed to punish honest mistakes, reasonable explanations, or accidents that are acknowledged and apologized for. Context matters.

01 How Strikes Are Issued

A strike may only be issued by the president or an HR member.

The president determines the severity of the offense and the corresponding point deduction.

HR records the member, the offense, the date, and the point deduction in the official log.

02 Five-Strike Scale
Strike Level Examples Action
Strike 1 Minor / first-time Late task without notice, minor club-room violation Logged warning and informal conversation with the department leader.
Strike 2 Repeated minor or one moderate offense Repeated minor behavior or a moderate breach Written warning from HR.
Strike 3 Continued pattern or serious offense Repeated misconduct or a serious breach Formal meeting with the department leader and HR.
Strike 4 Serious or repeated after Strike 3 Serious breach or continued behavior after the formal meeting President is notified and a formal warning is issued.
Strike 5 Final breach Final breach of the scale Membership review by the president, vice president, and HR.
03 Membership Review & Removal

At Strike 5, the member is reviewed by the president, vice president, and HR.

Removal is not permanent. A removed member may reapply after one full year.

A sixth offense following the review, or an offense serious enough to bypass the normal scale, results in immediate removal.

04 Semester Reset

Strikes reset every semester. Every member begins the new semester with a clean active strike count.

Records are still kept internally by HR. After the reset, the previous history remains visible to the president and HR and may be considered as context when necessary.

05 Important Clarifications

Strikes are for deliberate or repeated behavior, not misunderstandings, honest mistakes, or situations that were properly communicated and resolved.

An accidental issue that is acknowledged and apologized for does not automatically result in a strike.

Leaders use the same strike scale as all members, but are held to a higher standard because of their responsibilities.

PART IX

Point System

Earning, spending, balances, the red line and end-of-semester recognition.

+

The point system rewards members who stay active and contribute to the club. Points can be saved, spent, used as a safety net, and form part of the member's end-of-semester record.

01 Earning Points
Action Points
Complete an assigned task 2
Teach a skill to other members 2
Attend or participate in an event 3

Points are awarded by the department leader and logged with the member, action, and date.

The president has visibility across all departments and all point logs.

02 Spending Points
Use Cost Approval
Skip one task 5 points Department leader
Request removal of one strike 10 points President or HR

A task-skip request is not guaranteed. The member submits a request and the department leader accepts or denies it.

Strike removal is also a request, not an automatic exchange. The president or HR makes the final decision, and the 10 points are deducted only if the request is approved.

03 Three Point Values

Total: the member's lifetime cumulative points. It normally only increases. It decreases only when previous-semester credits are used to escape the red line.

Available: the points available during the current semester. This value resets to zero at the beginning of every semester.

Remaining: lifetime Total minus all points that have been spent or deducted.

04 Strikes & Point Deductions

The president determines the point deduction associated with a strike according to the severity of the offense.

The deduction is taken from the member's Available points.

A strike deducts its assigned points automatically. If the member later wants to request removal of that strike, an additional 10 points are required if the removal request is approved.

05 Red Line

A member crosses the red line when their Available points are less than or equal to zero.

If a member is already at or below zero when a new strike is issued, that new offense counts as a double strike.

Available points may become negative. The member must work back upward by completing tasks, teaching skills, or participating in events.

Further offenses while in the red-line state carry heavier consequences.

06 Previous Semester Credits — Safety Net

If a new deduction would push the member below zero, the system immediately checks the Remaining points from the immediately previous semester only.

The member may be prompted to use enough of those previous-semester credits to bring the current Available value exactly to zero, never above zero.

Credits used this way are deducted from the previous semester's Remaining value and from the lifetime Total. This is the only situation in which lifetime Total decreases.

If the previous semester does not contain enough Remaining points to reach zero, the member stays below zero and enters the red-line state.

07 End-Semester Recognition

At the end of each semester, members may be shown on a leaderboard based on raw points earned during that semester, before deductions.

The leaderboard is for recognition only and does not carry prizes.

The member's full point history remains archived and carries forward in their profile.

PART X

Platform Features Overview

How the rulebook's systems translate into the CMS.

+

This section is a reference for how the rulebook's systems are represented on the platform. It describes platform behavior and access; it does not replace the rules themselves.

01 Member Profiles

Each member profile displays the three point values: Total, Available, and Remaining.

The profile should display Total prominently, together with the current-semester or current-year context, and show Remaining and Available clearly.

Strike logs are visible to the member concerned, their department leader, HR, and the president.

Semester history is archived. Past totals, remaining balances, and strike history remain visible to the president and HR.

02 Point Management

Department leaders can award points to members of their own department by selecting the relevant action. The corresponding point value is filled automatically.

The president has full read and write visibility across all departments.

Every point entry is logged.

Members can submit a task-skip request through the platform. The department leader accepts or denies it.

Members can also submit a strike-removal request. The president or HR handles the request, and points are deducted only after approval.

03 Strike Management

The president and HR can issue strikes. The president determines the severity and point deduction.

If a strike deduction would take Available points below zero, the platform checks the immediately previous semester and prompts the member when eligible previous-semester credits exist.

Credits used from the previous semester reduce both the previous semester's Remaining balance and the lifetime Total.

If the member is already at or below zero when a new strike is issued, the platform applies the double-strike rule automatically.

Red-line status is visible to the president, HR, and the member's department leader.

04 Access Levels
Role Platform Access
Member Own profile, own points, own strikes, and the ability to send task-skip and strike-removal requests.
Department Leader Own department members; award and deduct points; accept or deny task-skip requests.
HR Issue strikes, handle strike-removal requests, and access all member logs.
President Full access to all logs and requests; issue strikes, set deductions, and confirm removals.

PART XI

Presidential Term

Term limits, succession and transition of responsibilities.

+
01 Presidential Term

The president may serve for a maximum of one academic year. A new presidential election is held at the end of that year.

The outgoing president may recommend a successor, but the recommendation is non-binding and does not replace the election process.

A proper transition must take place between the outgoing president, the incoming president, and the vice president. This includes transferring access, records, responsibilities, ongoing matters, and the institutional knowledge necessary to continue the club's work.

A former president remains a member in good standing and may serve the club in another role if elected or appointed to that role.

No individual may serve as president for more than one consecutive academic year.
